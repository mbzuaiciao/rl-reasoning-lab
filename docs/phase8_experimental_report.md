## 1. Which Prior Claims Were Most Reproducible?

Across our standardized replication harness ($n=12$ paired seeds with 95% percentile bootstrap confidence intervals):
1. **CLAIM-01 (Baseline Variance Reduction)**: State-value baseline reduces Monte Carlo policy gradient variance from $12.70 \to 3.08$ ($\Delta = -9.62$, 95% CI: $[-10.25, -8.94]$, Cohen's $d = -10.55$).
2. **CLAIM-05 (Proxy Verifier Goodhart Divergence)**: Optimization against imperfect verifiers reliably collapses true task utility to $22.7\%$ while driving proxy satisfaction to $94.3\%$ ($\Delta = -71.6\%$, Cohen's $d = -20.79$).
3. **CLAIM-03 (SFT Warm-Start)**: Warm-starting with supervised reasoning traces cuts time-to-convergence from $1,480 \to 199$ episodes ($\Delta = -1,281$, Cohen's $d = -9.75$) and eliminates early policy entropy collapse.
4. **CLAIM-02 (PPO Clipping Limits Drift)**: Clipped surrogate bounds empirical KL divergence below $0.08$, whereas unclipped policy updates explode past $1.85$ ($\Delta = -1.83$, Cohen's $d = -7.33$).

All four claims replicated robustly across every tested seed, exhibiting standardized effect sizes $|d| > 7.0$.

## 2. Which Claims Were Seed-Sensitive?

Empirical policy training without dense step rewards or KL regularization was heavily seed-sensitive:
- In `phase8_seed_sensitivity.py` ($n=20$ seeds), final policy accuracy ranged from **13.9%** (catastrophic collapse seed) to **95.0%** (lucky convergence seed), with an overall mean of **76.4%** and a standard deviation of **23.2%**.
- Across the 20 seeds:
  - 65% of seeds reached high reasoning accuracy ($>85\%$).
  - 25% of seeds fell into shortcut local optima ($65-70\%$).
  - 10% of seeds suffered complete early entropy collapse ($<20\%$).
- If an experimenter runs only $n=3$ seeds, the resulting 95% confidence interval is artificially tight yet highly volatile: $[82.5\%, 91.4\%]$ ($n=3$) compared to the true 20-seed interval $[65.3\%, 85.2\%]$. Three seeds can completely miss the 10% probability catastrophic failure mode.

## 3. Which Depended on Hyperparameter Budget?

In `phase8_hyperparameter_fairness.py`, we demonstrated that hyperparameter search budget disparity creates powerful illusions of algorithmic superiority:
- When Method A was granted **20 tuning configurations** and Method B was granted **2 configurations** on a task where both had **identical true capability (75.0%)**:
  - Method A reported best: **84.42%**
  - Method B reported best: **77.87%**
  - Apparent advantage of Method A: **+6.55%** (a pure artifact of search intensity!).
- When both methods were granted equal search budgets (20 configs each), Method A scored **84.42%** and Method B scored **84.46%** ($\Delta = -0.04\%$, statistically indistinguishable).
- Furthermore, the **Winner's Curse** was empirically verified: the configuration that achieved the highest validation score (averaging $78.12\%$) regressed to **70.08%** on fresh held-out test data (an empirical shrinkage of **-8.04%**).

---

## 4. Did Method Rankings Change Under Compute Matching?

Yes. In `phase8_compute_matching.py`, algorithm rankings completely inverted depending on the resource denominator:
1. **Under Equal Environment Interactions (4,000 samples)**:
   - Method B (Rejection Sampling / Replay) reached **64.9%**.
   - Method A (On-Policy PPO) reached **36.9%**.
   - **Winner**: **Method B by +28.0%**.
2. **Under Equal Optimizer Updates (500 steps)**:
   - Method A (On-Policy PPO) reached **87.6%**.
   - Method B (Rejection Sampling / Replay) reached **10.9%**.
   - **Winner**: **Method A by +76.7%**.

**Takeaway**: Calling an algorithm "more efficient" without explicitly specifying whether the bottleneck is environment sampling, gradient updates, or wall-clock compute is fundamentally scientifically invalid.

---

## 5. Which Effects Were Practically Important?

We distinguish between statistical precision and practical significance:
- **Practically Massive**:
  - Process-based intermediate reward vs Outcome-only reward: path validity increased from $42.8\% \to 88.6\%$ ($\Delta = +45.8\%$, Cohen's $d = +9.88$). This prevents reasoning hallucinations.
  - Adaptive test-time Best-of-N: achieved $88.0\%$ accuracy with $57.6$ generated tokens per prompt, matching fixed $N=8$ ($144.0$ tokens) while saving **60% of inference compute**.
- **Statistically Significant but Practically Negligible**:
  - In large-sample evaluations ($N=10,000$ rollouts), minor prompt formatting perturbations produced statistically significant p-values ($p = 0.003$), but an effect size of only $\Delta = +0.2\%$ ($d = 0.04$), which is practically negligible.

## 6. Which Disappeared Under OOD Evaluation?

In `phase8_distribution_shift_matrix.py`, we evaluated policies across a 6-axis distribution shift matrix (IID, Deeper Reasoning $d=4-6$, Dense Distractors $k=5$, Unseen Symbols, Counterfactual Edges, and Adversarial Traces):
- **Outcome PPO completely collapsed**:
  - IID Accuracy: **88.0%** (outperforming SFT by +16.0%).
  - Deeper Reasoning Accuracy: **18.0%** (collapsing below SFT's 45.0%).
  - Adversarial Traces Accuracy: **12.0%** (collapsing below SFT's 40.0%).
  - Mean OOD Accuracy: **26.4%** (OOD / IID ratio of only 30.0%).
- **Process PPO + KL was robust**:
  - IID Accuracy: **85.0%**
  - Mean OOD Accuracy: **71.8%** (OOD / IID ratio of 84.5%).
  - Worst subgroup: **65.0%** (Adversarial Traces).

**Takeaway**: High IID test accuracy achieved by outcome RL is frequently a mirage bought at the expense of shortcut memorization that collapses under minimal distribution shift.

## 7. Did Any Earlier Conclusions Need to Be Downgraded?

Yes. Two headline claims were updated and appropriately qualified in the repository documentation:
1. **"Outcome PPO learns general reasoning" $\to$ DOWNGRADED**:
   Outcome PPO learns *answer generation shortcuts*, not robust multi-step reasoning procedures. Under graph depth shifts or distractor edges, its validity drops to 18–25%. The claim was qualified: outcome RL only produces valid reasoning when reinforced by step-level process supervision or dense verifier ensembles.
2. **"Single-model sampling variability (self-consistency) detects model errors" $\to$ DOWNGRADED**:
   Self-consistency sampling works well IID (AUROC 0.76), but under out-of-distribution shifts (such as cyclic distractors or unseen symbols), the policy's sampling distribution collapses into uniform overconfidence (AUROC drops to **0.58**, barely above coin-flip). Only **deep ensembles** maintaining epistemic parameter diversity remained robust (AUROC 0.81).

## 8. Did Interactions Explain Any Apparent Component Effects?

Yes. In `phase8_ablation_and_interactions.py`, a $2 \times 2$ factorial ablation revealed a strong **super-additive interaction** between Process Reward ($A$) and Reference KL Regularization ($B$):
- $\Delta_A$ (Process Reward alone): $+13.76\%$
- $\Delta_B$ (KL Regularization alone): $+4.00\%$
- Additive expectation ($\Delta_A + \Delta_B$): $+17.76\%$
- Observed Joint Gain ($\Delta_{AB}$): $+24.44\%$
- **Synergistic Interaction**: **$+6.68\%$** (95% CI: $[+4.2\%, +8.9\%]$).

**Why the interaction exists**: Process reward provides the directional gradient needed to discover multi-step valid traces, while KL regularization prevents the policy logits from drifting into extreme overconfidence. Without KL, process reward eventually suffers from drift; without process reward, KL keeps the policy tethered to an imperfect baseline. One-at-a-time ablations completely miss this synergy.

## 9. How Much Did Confidence Intervals Change With More Seeds?

In `phase8_seed_sensitivity.py`, scaling the seed count tightened uncertainty bounds and revealed the low-tail failure distribution:
- $n = 3$: Mean $86.3\%$, 95% CI $[82.5\%, 91.4\%]$ (Width = $8.9\%$).
- $n = 5$: Mean $83.8\%$, 95% CI $[75.6\%, 90.3\%]$ (Width = $14.7\%$, capturing moderate drops).
- $n = 10$: Mean $77.2\%$, 95% CI $[61.4\%, 88.7\%]$ (Width = $27.3\%$, first encountering a collapse seed).
- $n = 20$: Mean $76.4\%$, 95% CI $[65.3\%, 85.2\%]$ (Width = $19.9\%$, stabilized true parameter estimate).

At $n=3$, the CI completely excluded the true population mean ($76.4\%$) because all 3 initial seeds happened to be in the favorable 65% convergence mode.

## 10. How Much Exaggeration Came From Best-Seed Reporting?

Across 20 seeds:
- **Best Seed**: **95.0%**
- **Median Seed**: **84.9%**
- **Mean Across Seeds**: **76.4%**
- **Worst Seed**: **13.9%**

Reporting only the best seed inflated reported performance by **$+18.6\%$** over the empirical mean, and completely concealed the fact that 1 in 10 runs suffered catastrophic early collapse.

## 11. Which Experimental Practices Produced the Largest False Conclusions?

Ranked by false effect magnitude:
1. **Imperfect Verifier Optimization (Goodhart Blindness)**: manufactured an apparent $+71.6\%$ gain on proxy reward while true task utility plummeted by $-77.3\%$.
2. **Best-Seed Cherry-Picking**: manufactured an apparent $+18.6\%$ performance boost.
3. **Unequal Hyperparameter Search Budgets**: manufactured an apparent $+6.55\%$ gain purely through unequal random search trials.
4. **Test-Set Contamination Across Tuning Iterations**: manufactured an apparent $+4.72\%$ improvement through repeated decision feedback on test evaluation noise.
5. **Simpson's Paradox / Unstratified Confounding**: aggregate trace length had a positive correlation with accuracy ($r = +0.34$), but conditioning on task depth revealed that within every difficulty slice, longer traces were negatively correlated with correctness ($r = -0.26$).

## 12. Final Conceptual Question: What Minimum Evidence Should Be Required Before Claiming That a New RL or Reasoning Method Is Better?

Grounding this requirement in the empirical evidence produced across Phase 8, **no claim of algorithmic superiority should be accepted unless it satisfies the following 7 empirical criteria**:

1. **Symmetric Seed Replications ($\ge 10$ Seeds)**:
   Both the proposed method and the baseline must be evaluated on at least 10 identical, paired random seeds. A 3-seed evaluation is insufficient to detect bimodal failure modes.
2. **Non-Parametric Uncertainty Intervals**:
   All reported metrics must include 95% percentile bootstrap confidence intervals and paired difference intervals ($\Delta_i = M_A^{(i)} - M_B^{(i)}$) rather than isolated point estimates.
3. **Strict Compute and Budget Matching**:
   Comparisons must report performance against explicit resource denominators: equal environment interaction samples, equal optimizer steps, and equal inference token budgets.
4. **Equalized Hyperparameter Search Budgets**:
   If the proposed method was tuned over $K$ hyperparameter configurations, the baseline must be tuned over an equal budget of $K$ configurations.
5. **Zero Test-Set Contamination**:
   Hyperparameters and checkpoint selection must be conducted exclusively on training and validation splits. The test set must be queried strictly once at the end of the study.
6. **Multi-Axis OOD Robustness Evaluation**:
   Evaluation must report worst-subgroup performance across distribution shifts (e.g. depth, distractors, perturbations) rather than a single in-distribution aggregate average.
7. **Mechanistic and Control Verification**:
   The study must include negative controls (confirming that irrelevant changes produce null effects) and mechanism-oriented measurements (confirming that the proposed architectural change acted via its hypothesized latent mechanism).
